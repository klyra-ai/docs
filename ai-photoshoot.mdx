AI Photoshoot helps you turn product images into polished, marketing-ready visuals without needing a traditional photoshoot.

You can start with a custom creative direction or use a professionally designed template. Choose your product, control the aspect ratio and number of images, then generate a set of product visuals you can review and manage from **My Photoshoots**.

<Note>
AI Photoshoot is designed around outcomes. You do not need to understand the underlying image model or technical generation settings to create professional-looking product visuals.
</Note>

## What you can create

AI Photoshoot provides three creation modes:

- **Custom Photoshoot**: describe the scene and creative direction in your own words.
- **Template-Based Generation**: choose from curated templates and generate product photos quickly.
- **Background Replacement**: replace a product background with a new scene while preserving the product.

This tutorial focuses on the two creation workflows shown in the current Klyra AI interface: **Template-Based Generation** and **Custom Photoshoot**.

## Before you start

You will need:

1. A Klyra AI account.
2. A product image in your product library, or a product image you can upload.
3. A clear idea of the type of product visual you want to create.

<Info>
For best results, use a clear product image where the product is easy to see. A clean source image gives the generation system more information to work with.
</Info>

## Template-Based Generation

Template-Based Generation lets you select a product and then choose a visual template designed for a specific marketing scenario.

### Step 1: Start a new photoshoot

Open **Template-Based Generation** from the AI Photoshoot home page.

The setup panel appears on the left, while available template categories and templates appear on the right.

<Frame caption="Template-Based Generation setup">
  <img src="/images/ai-photoshoot/02-template-based-generation.webp" alt="Klyra AI Template-Based Generation screen showing product selection, template categories, aspect ratio, image count, and notes" />
</Frame>

### Step 2: Select your product

In the **Setup** panel, select **Product**.

The selected product is displayed in the setup panel. The product becomes the visual reference for the generated photos.

<Info>
If the correct product is not selected, choose the product before selecting the final template so you can preview the workflow with the intended product context.
</Info>

### Step 3: Choose a template category

The template picker includes categories such as:

- **Studio**
- **In Use**
- **Lifestyle**
- **UGC**
- **Billboard**
- **Front View**
- **Flatlay**
- **Editorial**
- **Composition**
- **Ingredient-Based**
- **Floating**
- **Contextual**

Each category is intended for a different visual outcome.

For example:

- **Studio** works well for clean product photography.
- **In Use** places the product into a realistic usage scenario.
- **Lifestyle** is useful for marketing visuals with a more natural, aspirational setting.
- **UGC** is suited to authentic user-generated-content style imagery.
- **Billboard** is designed for large-scale advertising visuals.
- **Flatlay** works well for top-down product compositions.
- **Editorial** is suited to premium magazine-style product imagery.

### Step 4: Pick a specific template

After selecting a category, choose one of the available templates.

The template cards show the visual concept before generation. In the example screenshot, the **UGC** category contains templates such as:

- **Selfie Holding**
- **Unboxing Moment**
- **Bathroom Shelf**
- **Car Passenger**
- **On the Go**
- **Tutorial Frame**

Select the template that best matches the marketing context you want.

### Step 5: Choose the aspect ratio

AI Photoshoot supports these aspect ratios:

- **1:1**
- **4:5**
- **5:4**
- **4:3**
- **3:4**
- **16:9**
- **9:16**

Choose the ratio based on where you intend to use the image.

<Info>
A practical rule is to choose the output format before generating. Portrait ratios are useful for vertical placements, while landscape ratios are better suited to wider layouts. Use 1:1 when you want a flexible square asset.
</Info>

### Step 6: Choose the number of images

You can generate:

- **1** image
- **2** images
- **3** images
- **4** images

Generating multiple images gives you more creative variations to choose from.

### Step 7: Add additional notes

The **Additional notes** field is optional.

Use it when you want to provide extra creative direction, props, wording, or other context that should influence the result.

For example:

> Keep the product clearly visible and use natural lighting with a realistic everyday setting.

Keep notes focused on the outcome you want.

### Step 8: Generate the photoshoot

Once the product, template, aspect ratio, image count, and optional notes are ready, select **Generate Photoshoot**.

Klyra AI generates the requested product visuals and displays them in the results area.

<Note>
Generation time can vary depending on the selected workflow and current processing load.
</Note>

## Custom Photoshoot

Custom Photoshoot gives you more control over the scene because you describe the desired visual directly.

### Step 1: Open Custom Photoshoot

From the AI Photoshoot home page, select **Custom Photoshoot**.

The page is divided into two main areas:

- **Setup** on the left
- **Generated Results** on the right

<Frame caption="Custom Photoshoot">
  <img src="/images/ai-photoshoot/03-custom-photoshoot.webp" alt="Klyra AI Custom Photoshoot screen showing creative direction, product selection, generation settings, and generated results" />
</Frame>

### Step 2: Write your creative direction

The **Creative direction** field is required.

Describe what you want the generated product photos to show.

A strong creative direction can include:

- Who or what is using the product
- The environment
- The action or pose
- Lighting
- Camera feel
- Mood
- Clothing or styling when relevant
- Product placement
- Important visual constraints

For a footwear product, for example:

> Stylish young adult man wearing the selected athletic sneaker. Show him walking naturally on a modern urban street, with the selected sneaker clearly visible and in focus. Use contemporary casual clothing, soft natural daylight, realistic shadows, and a premium commercial photography look.

<Info>
When the product is the focus, explicitly tell the AI to keep it visible, recognizable, and in focus. This helps make the generated visual more useful for product marketing.
</Info>

### Step 3: Select your product

Under **Pick your product**, choose the product that should guide generation.

The interface supports product selection from **My Products** and also provides options to **Upload** or **Generate** a product.

You can select up to three products when the workflow supports multiple product references.

For a single-product campaign, select the product you want to feature.

### Step 4: Configure lighting

Use the **Lighting** control to guide the lighting style.

The screenshot shows the default **Auto** setting.

Use the default when you want Klyra AI to determine suitable lighting automatically.

### Step 5: Configure the camera angle

Use **Camera Angle** to control the visual viewpoint.

The screenshot shows the default **Auto** setting.

Keep it on Auto when your creative direction already describes the desired scene and you want the system to choose an appropriate viewpoint.

### Step 6: Choose the aspect ratio

Select one of the available aspect ratios:

- **1:1**
- **4:5**
- **5:4**
- **4:3**
- **3:4**
- **16:9**
- **9:16**

In the example shown, **4:5** is selected for a portrait-oriented product campaign.

### Step 7: Choose the number of images

Select how many variations you want:

- **1**
- **2**
- **3**
- **4**

In the example screenshot, **4** images are selected, producing four variations in the generated results area.

### Step 8: Generate the photoshoot

When the setup is complete, select **Generate Photoshoot**.

The generated images appear under **Generated Results**.

Review the variations and choose the images that best match your intended campaign or content.

## How to write better creative directions

The quality of a custom photoshoot starts with the direction you provide.

A useful structure is:

```text
Subject + action + environment + product focus + lighting + visual style
```

For example:

```text
A stylish young adult man wearing the selected athletic sneaker,
walking naturally on a modern urban street. Keep the sneaker clearly
visible and in focus. Use soft natural daylight, realistic shadows,
and a premium commercial photography style.
```

### Be specific about the product

Instead of:

> Create a nice shoe photo.

Try:

> Create a premium lifestyle photo featuring the selected athletic sneaker. Keep the sneaker clearly visible and in sharp focus.

### Describe the environment

Instead of:

> Put the product outside.

Try:

> Place the subject on a modern urban street with contemporary buildings, natural pedestrian activity, and realistic daylight.

### Describe the intended visual outcome

Instead of:

> Make it look professional.

Try:

> Use a polished commercial photography aesthetic with realistic lighting, natural shadows, and a clean premium composition.

<Warning>
Avoid adding unnecessary instructions that do not contribute to the desired visual. Clear, focused directions are generally easier to translate into a consistent product image.
</Warning>

## Review generated results

After generation, the results appear in the **Generated Results** section.

For Custom Photoshoot, multiple variations can be displayed together so you can compare them quickly.

Look for:

- Accurate product appearance
- Clear product visibility
- Natural positioning
- Realistic lighting
- Appropriate composition
- Consistency with your creative direction
- Suitability for your intended marketing channel

If the result does not match your intent, adjust the creative direction or generation settings and create another photoshoot.

## Recommended workflow

For most product campaigns, this workflow is a good starting point:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add your product">
    Make sure the product image is available in your product library or upload the product you want to use.
  </Step>

</Steps>

## Common issues

### The generated image does not look like the selected product

Make the product reference clear and strengthen the product-specific instruction in your creative direction.

For example:

> Keep the selected sneaker's shape, colors, materials, and overall design consistent with the product reference.

### The product is difficult to see

Specify that the product should be clearly visible and in focus. You can also adjust the scene description so the product is not obscured by people, props, or the environment.

### The scene is not what you expected

Use Custom Photoshoot and describe the environment, subject, action, lighting, and composition more specifically.

### You need a different format

Create another photoshoot using the required aspect ratio. Choose the output format before generation whenever possible.

### You want more options

Increase the number of images from 1 to 2, 3, or 4 and compare the generated variations.
