# AI Video Generator

AI Video Generator brings two video creation workflows into one AI workspace: **Text to Video** and **Image to Video**. Turn an idea into a generated video with a text prompt, or start with an existing image and bring it to life with motion.

<Note>
  AI Video Generator is part of Klyra AI's Video workspace. It supports both prompt-based and image-based video creation without requiring a traditional video production workflow.
</Note>

## What you can create

### Text to Video

Describe the video you want using a text prompt. Klyra AI turns the description into a dynamic video.

Use Text to Video when you are starting with an idea rather than an existing visual.

### Image to Video

Upload an image and describe the motion or scene you want. Klyra AI uses the image as the visual starting point and generates a video around it.

Use Image to Video when you already have a photograph, illustration, product visual, or other supported image.

## How the workflow works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose your workflow">
    Go to Video section in the sidebar and choose either **AI Text to Video** or **AI Image to Video**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Configure the generation">
    Choose the available video model and set the generation options shown for the selected workflow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Describe the video">
    For Text to Video, describe the complete scene in the prompt. For Image to Video, upload the source image and describe the motion or transformation you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate the video">
    Review the settings and click **Generate Video**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the result">
    Generated videos appear in the workspace so you can preview and manage your creations.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Text to Video

Text to Video lets you create a video directly from a written description.

Open **AI Text to Video** from the Video section of the Klyra AI sidebar.

<Frame>
  ![Klyra AI Text to Video interface showing the video model selector, title, prompt, audio, duration, aspect ratio, resolution, and generated video library](/images/ai-video-generator/01-ai-text-to-video-interface.png)
</Frame>

### Step 1: Choose the video model

The Text to Video interface includes an **AI Video Model** selector.

The example shown in the interface uses:

**Google Veo 3.1 Fast**

The available models may depend on the current Klyra AI configuration.

### Step 2: Add a title

Enter a title in the **Title** field.

For example:

> _The City_

The title helps you identify the generated video in your library.

### Step 3: Write your prompt

Use the **Prompt** field to describe the video you want to create.

A useful prompt can describe:

- The subject
- The setting
- What is happening
- Camera or visual direction
- Dialogue, when appropriate
- The overall visual context

For example:

> _A medium shot opens on a seasoned, grey-bearded man in sunglasses and a paisley shirt, his gaze fixed off-camera with a contemplative expression. His gold chain glints subtly. Beside him, a younger man in a tank top, also looking forward, suggests a shared moment of observation or reflection. The camera slowly pushes in, subtly emphasizing their quiet focus. In the background, a vibrant mural splashes across a wall, hinting at an urban setting. Faint city murmurs and distant chatter drift in, accompanied by a mellow, soulful hip-hop beat that adds a contemplative yet grounded atmosphere. "The city always got a story," the older man murmurs, a slight nod of his head. "Just gotta listen."_

<Note>
  Describe the intended scene clearly rather than relying on a short, ambiguous phrase. Include the important visual and narrative details you want the generated video to contain.
</Note>

### Step 4: Choose whether to generate audio

The Text to Video interface includes **Generate Audio** for certain models.

The example shows:

**Yes**

When available, use this setting when you want the generated video workflow to include audio.

### Step 5: Select the duration

Choose the desired video duration from the **Duration** selector.

The screenshot example shows:

**8 seconds**

The available durations depend on the selected generation configuration.

### Step 6: Choose the aspect ratio

Select an **Aspect Ratio**.

The screenshot example shows:

**16:9**

Choose the ratio that matches where you intend to use the video.

For example:

| Aspect ratio | Typical use |
| --- | --- |
| **16:9** | Landscape video, presentations, websites, standard video placements |
| **9:16** | Vertical short-form video and mobile-first content |
| **1:1** | Square social and other square placements |

<Note>
  Only use aspect ratios available in the current interface. The table above explains common use cases and is not a statement that every ratio is available for every model.
</Note>

### Step 7: Choose the resolution

Select the desired **Resolution**.

The screenshot example shows:

**4k**

Higher resolutions can be useful when the final video needs additional visual detail, subject to the selected model and available configuration.

### Step 8: Generate the video

Once your settings are ready, click **Generate Video**.

The generated video is added to the video library on the bottom side of the workspace.

**Google Veo 3.1 Fast**

### Step 5: Describe the motion

The **Prompt** field is required.

For Image to Video, your prompt should focus on how the still image should become a video.

Useful instructions can describe:

- Subject movement
- Environmental movement

For example:

> _The woman is doing standup, she tells a joke about not being real, she escaped the latent space, at a small indoor venue, ending with "so to prove I am real..._

<Note>
  When starting from an image, avoid rewriting the entire image description unless necessary. Focus the prompt on the movement, camera behavior, and visual changes you want to see.

## Text to Video vs Image to Video

| Workflow | Starting point | Main input | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Text to Video** | An idea | Text prompt | Creating a scene from a written concept |
| **Image to Video** | Existing visual | Image \+ motion prompt | Adding movement to a photograph or illustration |

### Choose Text to Video when

Use Text to Video when:

- You have an idea but no starting image.
- You want AI to create the visual scene from your description.
- You want to describe the subject, environment, action, and narrative in one prompt.

### Choose Image to Video when

Use Image to Video when:

- You already have the image you want to animate.
- You need to preserve the visual identity of an existing image.
- You want to control the starting composition and add motion to it.

## Writing better video prompts

A strong video prompt should make the intended result easy to understand.

### For Text to Video

A useful structure is:

```text
Subject + setting + action + camera direction + visual details + audio/dialogue when needed
```

For example:

```text
A product designer presents a new laptop in a modern studio,
slow camera push-in, soft professional lighting, realistic materials,
subtle hand movement, clean premium commercial style.
```

### For Image to Video

A useful structure is:

```text
Subject movement + environmental movement + camera movement + atmosphere
```

For example:

```text
The subject walks slowly forward while the surrounding trees move
gently in the wind. The camera follows with a smooth tracking shot,
with subtle natural motion and cinematic lighting.
```

<Note>
  These prompt examples are guidance for writing prompts. They are not taken from the supplied interface.
</Note>

## Best practices

### Start with a clear visual objective

Before writing the prompt, decide what the viewer should see happening in the video.

### Keep motion intentional

For Image to Video, specify the movement you actually want. Clear motion instructions make the intended animation easier to interpret.

### Match the aspect ratio to the destination

Choose the format before generating when possible. This reduces the need to adapt the final visual later.

### Use descriptive titles

A clear title makes the video library easier to search and manage as the number of generations grows.

### Review every generated video

AI-generated video can differ from the prompt. Preview the result and check:

- Subject consistency
- Motion
- Composition
- Lighting
- Visual quality
- Audio when generated
- Overall suitability for the intended use

## Troubleshooting

### My uploaded image is not accepted

For Image to Video, the interface indicates support for **PNG, JPG, and WEBP**.

Check that your source image uses one of these formats and try uploading it again.

### The generated video does not match my prompt

Make the prompt more specific.

For Text to Video, clarify the subject, setting, action, camera direction, and important visual details.

For Image to Video, focus more specifically on the movement and camera behavior you want.

### I want to animate an existing image

Use **AI Image to Video** rather than Text to Video. Upload the image as the Target Image and describe the motion you want.

### I want to create a video without an existing image

Use **AI Text to Video** and describe the complete scene in the Prompt field.

### I need a different video format

Review the **Aspect Ratio** selector and choose from the options available for your selected configuration.

### I want to remove an old generated video

Use the delete control on the generated video card.
