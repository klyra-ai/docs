[AI Music Studio](https://klyra.ai/apps/ai-music-generator) turns a simple musical idea into an original track, then gives you the controls to shape, organize, and mix your creations in one workspace.

You can start with a fast prompt, build a song section by section with Composition Plan, manage every generated track in your Library, and combine tracks in the multi-track Mixer.

<Note>
  AI Music Studio is part of Klyra AI, the AI Operating System. The goal is to keep music creation simple while giving you deeper controls when you need them.
</Note>

## What you can do

With AI Music Studio, you can:

- Generate music from a text prompt.
- Build a song section by section with Composition Plan.
- Control song length, vibe, mood, vocals, styles, and advanced generation settings.
- Create instrumental or vocal tracks.
- Add positive and negative global style tags.
- Choose a generation model and optional seed.
- Browse, search, favorite, archive, download, and delete generated tracks.
- Play tracks from the persistent audio player.
- Stack multiple AI tracks in the multi-track Mixer.
- Adjust volume, pan, fades, and start timing for individual layers.
- Apply master volume, reverb, and lowpass processing.
- Save and recall named mixes.

The feature set described above is part of the AI Music Studio product specification. 

## AI Music Studio at a glance

The workspace has three main areas:

1. **Studio** for creating music.
2. **Library** for managing generated tracks.
3. **Mixer** for combining and balancing tracks.

The product specification describes the Studio as a complete in-browser music studio, the Library as a searchable archive, and the Mixer as a multi-track workspace for balancing layers and bouncing a master mix. 

## Create your first track

Open **AI Music Studio** from Klyra AI.

The Studio opens with the music composer at the top and your recent tracks below it.

<Frame>
  ![AI Music Studio Prompt mode showing the music prompt composer, creative controls, Generate button, and Recent tracks](/images/ai-music-studio/01-studio-prompt-mode.webp)
</Frame>

### Step 1: Choose Prompt mode

The Studio provides two creation modes:

- **Prompt**
- **Composition Plan**

Use **Prompt** when you want to describe the overall sound and let AI handle the composition.

Use **Composition Plan** when you want more direct control over sections, lyrics, styles, and timing.

The product specification identifies Prompt mode as the fast text-to-music workflow and Composition Plan as the section-by-section workflow. 

### Step 2: Write your music prompt

Describe the track you want to hear.

A useful prompt can include:

- Genre or musical style
- Instruments
- Mood
- Vocal preference
- Tempo
- Production characteristics
- Overall atmosphere

For example:

> A dreamy lo-fi hip-hop track with mellow piano, jazzy chords, vinyl crackle, soft drums and a chill vibe at 80 bpm.

The Studio interface provides a large prompt field with a character counter and a **Surprise me** option for generating a curated example prompt. 

<Note>
  You do not need to write a technically complex prompt. Describe the sound, feeling, instruments, and energy you want.
</Note>

### Step 3: Set the track length

Use the length control to choose how long the generated track should be.

The product specification describes quick length presets including:

- Hook
- Loop
- Verse
- Short
- Full
- Extended

The exact presets available can depend on the current Studio configuration. 

### Step 4: Choose a vibe

Use **Vibe** to guide the overall musical direction.

The product specification lists visual vibe categories including:

- Cinematic
- Lo-Fi
- Electronic
- Ambient
- Orchestral
- Rock
- Jazz
- Hip-Hop
- Classical

It also describes a full genre browser for additional choices. 

### Step 5: Choose a mood

Use **Mood** to influence the emotional character of the track.

Choose a mood that matches the feeling you want the finished music to communicate.

### Step 6: Choose vocals or instrumental

Use **Vocals** to control whether the generated track includes vocals or is instrumental.

The product specification describes this as an Instrumental vs Vocals toggle. 

### Step 7: Open Advanced controls when needed

Use **Advanced** when you need additional control over generation.

The product specification lists:

- Custom track title or automatic title from the prompt
- Multiple output formats such as MP3 and WAV
- Selectable generation model
- Optional seed for reproducible results

Advanced controls should be used when you need specific output behavior. For a first track, the default settings are usually the simplest place to start. 

### Step 8: Generate

Click **Generate**.

AI Music Studio processes the request and creates your track.

The product specification describes a fullscreen generation progress experience with an animated visualizer and real-time AI Capacity information in the top bar. 

<Note>
  Generation uses your available AI Capacity. Klyra AI presents capacity to users rather than exposing its internal credit systems as the primary experience. 
</Note>

## Use Composition Plan for more control

If you want to design the structure of a song yourself, switch from **Prompt** to **Composition Plan**.

<Frame>
  ![AI Music Studio Composition Plan showing the timeline area, global styles, and Add section controls](/images/ai-music-studio/02-studio-composition-plan.webp)
</Frame>

Composition Plan lets you sketch the song before generating it.

### Auto-plan a song

Click **Auto-plan from prompt** to turn your existing prompt into a song structure.

This is useful when you know the sound you want but do not want to manually build every section.

The product specification explicitly supports automatically planning a full song layout from a prompt.

### Add sections manually

Use **Add section** to build your song timeline.

You can add sections such as:

- Intro
- Verse
- Chorus
- Bridge
- Other supported song sections

The Composition Plan is designed for adding, editing, and reordering sections while tracking the total duration.

### Configure each section

A section can be configured with its own:

- Lyrics
- Length
- Styling

As you build the timeline, the total duration updates so you can understand the overall song structure.

### Use global styles

Composition Plan includes two style areas:

**Positive Global Styles**

Add styles you want the track to include.

Examples:

- Cinematic
- Warm
- Dreamy
- Analog

**Negative Global Styles**

Add styles or characteristics you want the generation to avoid.

Examples:

- Harsh
- Distorted
- Heavy metal

The product specification describes positive and negative global style tags as a way to steer or block sounds.

<Note>
  Use positive and negative styles together when you have a clear creative direction. Keep them focused rather than adding a long list of unrelated instructions.
</Note>

## Play your music

AI Music Studio includes a persistent audio player.

The player provides:

- Play and pause
- Seek through the track
- Rewind and forward controls
- Volume control
- Download

The product specification describes 15-second rewind and forward shortcuts and a custom waveform progress bar.

<Note>
  The player remains available while you work elsewhere in the Studio, making it easier to compare or listen while continuing your workflow.
</Note>

## Manage your music in Library

Open **Library** from the top navigation.

The Library is your central collection of generated tracks.

<Frame>
  ![AI Music Studio Library showing search, All Favorites Archived filters, a generated track card, and the persistent audio player](/images/ai-music-studio/03-music-library.webp)
</Frame>

### Search your tracks

Use the search field to find tracks by:

- Title
- Prompt

The product specification describes a debounced live search for the generated track archive.

### Filter your collection

The Library provides filters for:

- **All**
- **Favorites**
- **Archived**

Use these filters to quickly narrow the collection.

### Manage individual tracks

Each track card can include actions for:

- Favorite
- Download
- Archive
- Delete

The product specification also describes track cards containing information such as genre, mood, instrumental status, length, and prompt preview.

<Warning>
  Use Delete only when you are sure you no longer need a track. Archiving is a better choice when you want to remove a track from your active collection without treating it as a permanent deletion.
</Warning>

### Load more tracks

For larger collections, the Library supports load-more pagination.

This allows your music collection to grow without requiring everything to appear on the screen at once. 

## Build a multi-track mix

Open **Mixer** from the top navigation.

The Multi-track Mixer lets you combine AI-generated tracks as independent layers.

<Frame>
  ![AI Music Studio Multi-track Mixer showing the track browser, mixer layer controls, playback controls, and master controls](/images/ai-music-studio/04-multi-track-mixer.webp)
</Frame>

The mixer is designed to let you stack tracks, balance their levels, adjust timing, and process the final mix. 

### Step 1: Find a track

Use the **Your Tracks** panel on the left.

Search your music collection and find the track you want to add.

Click the **\+** button to add it to the mix.

### Step 2: Add multiple layers

Repeat the process to build your mix.

Each track appears as an independent layer in the main mixer.

The maximum number of simultaneous layers is configurable by the product implementation. 

### Step 3: Adjust volume

Use the **Volume** control for each layer to balance the relative level of the track.

For example:

- Lower a background layer.
- Raise a melody layer.
- Reduce a vocal layer if it is overpowering other elements.

### Step 4: Adjust pan

Use **Pan** to position a layer within the stereo field.

This can help separate layers and create a wider mix.

### Step 5: Add fade-in and fade-out

Use:

- **Fade In**
- **Fade Out**

to create smoother entrances and exits for individual layers.

### Step 6: Set the start time

Use the **Start** control to determine when a layer enters the mix.

This is useful when one track should begin after another track has already started.

The product specification lists per-layer start-time control as part of the Mixer. 

### Step 7: Use Mute and Solo

Each layer provides **M** and **S** controls.

Use:

- **Mute** to temporarily silence a layer.
- **Solo** to hear a layer in isolation while evaluating the mix.

These controls make it easier to compare individual parts without removing them from the project.

### Step 8: Reorder layers

Use the up and down controls to change the layer order.

The product specification supports one-click layer reordering. 

## Adjust the master mix

The bottom of the Mixer contains master controls.

### Master Volume

Controls the overall output level of the mix.

### Reverb

Adds or reduces the master reverb effect.

### Lowpass

Controls the master lowpass filter.

The Mixer specification identifies master volume, reverb, and lowpass as the master-bus controls. 

<Note>
  Make small changes and listen after each adjustment. The goal is a balanced mix, not simply the loudest possible output.
</Note>

## Play and save your mix

Use the Mixer controls to:

- **Play mix**
- **Stop**
- **Save**
- **New**

The product specification describes real-time playback through the Web Audio API and support for saving, loading, and recalling named mixes. 

### Save a mix

Enter a name for your mix and click **Save**.

Saved mixes can be recalled later.

### Start a new mix

Use **New** when you want to begin a separate mix.

## Tips for better results

### Describe the sound, not the technology

Instead of trying to specify how AI should generate the music, describe the result you want to hear.

Include things such as:

- Genre
- Instrumentation
- Mood
- Tempo
- Energy
- Vocal preference
- Production character

### Keep prompts focused

A focused prompt gives you a clearer creative direction.

If you want a significant change, change one or two important characteristics at a time and compare the results.

### Use Composition Plan for song structure

Prompt mode is ideal for speed.

Composition Plan is better when the arrangement itself matters, especially when you need specific sections, lyrics, lengths, and timing.

### Use negative styles intentionally

Negative global styles are useful when the generator repeatedly introduces a sound or characteristic you do not want.

### Organize your Library

Favorite strong tracks and archive tracks that are not currently part of your active work.

This keeps the Library easier to navigate as your collection grows.

### Mix with small adjustments

When mixing multiple layers, make gradual changes to volume, pan, fades, and timing. Listen to the result after each important adjustment.
